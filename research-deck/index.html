How to read this

Three grades. Never mix them.

Sourced

A named third party. Quotable with the source named. Most of this deck.

Modelled

Our construction from sourced inputs, with the assumptions written down. Shown as a range, never a point.

First-hand

Direct observation by a named team member, dated, role stated, bounded to what that person saw.

Two rules that protect every number here.
1. Models are not aircraft. Eleven DJI models could be 30 airframes or 300. Say models every time.
2. Nobody publishes airframe counts. Not the IDF, not the CAAI, not the Police. Any total is a modelled band and is drawn as one.

Why the discipline is the product. The strongest evidence we own is four people's testimony, and the strongest data is someone else's reporting. Credibility rests on how carefully both are stated — not on how many numbers are on the slide.

Growth — what we do not have

Six gaps, and the honest CAGR problem

The market CAGRs we can cite are not ours. Fleet-management software grows at 17.7–18.3% to 2036 and drone software at ~19.9% to 2035 — but that bundle is mostly compliance, media processing and manufacturer flight software. Applying it to the coordination slice is borrowed, not measured. Say so before someone says it for us.

What is sourced and safe: Israel's UAV market $557.6M (2025) → $855.7M (2030), 8.9% CAGR · commercial drone market $28.0B → $52.1B by 2033 · FAA certified pilots 493K → 628.6K by 2030 · global commercial UAS fleet → 5.6M by 2050 · defense-tech VC $49.1B in 2025, nearly double the prior year.

1 · No growth rate for the coordination layer. No analyst sizes it, so it has no CAGR of its own.

2 · No competitor growth series. Point-in-time revenue for nearly all of them. We do not know whether FlytBase's $10.8M is growing 5% or 60% — and that changes how slide 12 reads.

3 · No Israeli civilian fleet series. CAAI said ~20,000 in 2017 and has published nothing since. A nine-year gap, one request away from closing.

4 · No Israeli defense software spend series — we have the hardware trajectory only.

5 · Agriculture: nothing at all.

6 · No pricing trend. Whether ACVs rise or fall as the category matures is unanswered — and it decides whether our own revenue ladder holds.

Israeli incumbents11
The question we must answer before it is asked

A $2.5–3.9B category whose leading pure-play earns $10.8M

The bear reading. The analyst figure bundles compliance, media processing and manufacturer flight software. The genuine coordination slice is far smaller than the headline — and either the category is forming, or operators do not yet see the problem as software-shaped.

The bull reading, and it points at defense. eyesAtop reportedly does tens of millions selling coordination into Israeli defense with ~50 people, and XTEND holds a $20M Israeli MoD contract for a multi-drone operating system. Global civil pure-play ≈ $10.8M. Israeli defense, one vendor ≈ tens of millions. That is the strongest quantitative support defense-first has.

And the bear reading got worse on 19 Aug. FlytBase's headcount went from 98 (Sept 2025) to 48 (May 2026). Revenue reporting across databases is too contradictory to cite — $10.8M ARR against $4M, funding $1.6M against $156M — but a category leader roughly halving its headcount is not a category compounding. Say the headcount, not the revenue, and do not explain it away.

Where we sit

Four layers. They are bought separately.

Layer 3 has no incumbent that is simultaneously vendor-neutral at the aircraft, battery-feasibility-first, and not tied to its own hardware. That intersection is the claim — not "nobody does coordination," which is false and falsifiable in one search.

The stack, and who owns each layer14
The single most important picture we own

Everyone else shrinks a crew. We coordinate a crowd.

This is structural, not a feature. A competitor can add battery-aware planning in a quarter. No competitor can easily change what shape of fleet its product exists to serve. eyesAtop's entire value proposition is three or four soldiers become one — it exists to shrink a crew. Shrinking a crew does not coordinate a hundred soldiers flying one Mavic each.

Primary evidence17
What the data actually tells us

Six conclusions

1 · Mixed fleets are the normal case, and it is documented. 32 models, 5 manufacturers, three agencies. The heterogeneity argument is now a citation, not an assertion.

2 · Two adapters reach most of the estate. DJI + Autel = 81% of documented models, and independently the only two a founder saw in service. De-risking claim, and de-risking is what a pre-seed room listens for.

3 · The category is contested, not empty. Six funded companies sell multi-drone coordination as a primary product. "Almost nobody does this" is false and must be retired from every document and slide.

4 · But the volume tier is untouched. The best-funded competitor is fielded in specialist units and has not reached line units — confirmed by four people inside one. That tier is the fat part of the distribution.

5 · Defense is where the money is, and the data now says so. A 50-person company earns tens of millions in this exact category, in this country. A $20M MoD contract exists as a price anchor. Civilian public safety spends ₪10M a year on aircraft.

6 · Battery feasibility is the one uncontested claim — verified absent from the leading civil product's own documentation and from every description of the leading defense product.

Insights18
What we do about it

Five things, in order

1 · Write down the R&D unit conversation — this week. What was described, what he responded to, what he asked. That transcript is a validated product spec and it will not survive a week of memory. Then ask what "test" means to them before building anything.

2 · Build the refusal demo. The system declines a task an airframe cannot complete and returns, and proposes the one that can. Thirty seconds. No competitor demo refuses.

3 · Close the on-prem prerequisites. Mongo credentials, per-service secrets, auth on the telemetry ingest, and a values file that installs from a mirror with no internet. About a week — and the difference between "we can deploy on-prem" and "here it is, offline."

4 · Route it through MAFAT Green Lane. IP retained, 30-day terms, ISO exemption. An interested unit attached to the application is the hard part, and we have one.

5 · Fix two overstated claims before the next investor meeting. The national-coordination-layer role is licensed to High Lander, and manufacturer encroachment is no longer hypothetical — Auterion shipped it. Both are flagged in the market-sizing document and both are falsifiable in one search.
