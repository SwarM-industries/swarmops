## 1. Exit comparables — the table this company did not have

Investors price against exits, not against TAM. **No SwarmOps document contained a single exit
comparable until now.** [All SOURCED]

| Acquirer ← Target | Price | Date | What was bought | Capital raised before exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flock Safety ← Aerodome** | **$300M+** | Oct 2024 | Drone-as-first-responder **software** — autonomous launch on a 911 call, scene assessment | **$28M**, company was **17 months old** |
| **Ondas ← American Robotics** | **$70.6M** | 2021 | Drone-in-a-box site autonomy (Optimus, first FAA-certified sUAS for automated security) | — |
| **Ondas ← Airobotics** | **$15.2M** (all stock) | Jan 2023 | Israeli drone-in-a-box | IPO'd on TASE at ~**$130M**; valued **$240M** the year before |

### 1.1 The spread is the finding, and it maps onto our positioning

**Aerodome returned roughly 10× its capital in 17 months. Airobotics exited at ~6% of its prior
private valuation.** Same sector, same years, two orders of magnitude apart.

The difference is not execution quality — it is **what layer the company sold**:

- **Aerodome sold coordination software** into a buyer segment that was forming fast (DFR), owned
  no manufacturing, and was acquired by a company that needed the layer rather than the aircraft.
- **Airobotics and American Robotics sold hardware-anchored site autonomy** — own aircraft, own
  dock, own site. Capital-heavy, single-vendor, and priced accordingly.

**This is the strongest external evidence we have found for the layer thesis**
(`SwarmOps_Competitive_Battlecard.md` §3.6). It is a market pricing the coordination layer above
the hardware layer, in cash, twice.

**Use it carefully.** Aerodome was acquired for a **US public-safety** DFR position with an
a16z-to-a16z relationship behind it (both companies were a16z-backed; Flock had raised $680M+).
It is a comparable for *what layer gets paid*, not a prediction of our outcome. Say it that way.

## 2. Ondas — a correction we needed

`SwarmOps_Competitor_eyesAtop.md` records that eyesAtop + Kela beat "US rival Ondas" for the
Digital Bat contract. **Ondas is not a startup.** [SOURCED]

| | |
|---|---|
| Q2 2026 revenue | **$83.8M** |
| FY2026 guidance | **$525–550M** |
| Pro-forma backlog | **$757M** — up from **$68M** at Q4 2025 (**11×**) |
| Market cap | **$5.26B**, stock **+972%** over the year |
| Acquisitions in 2026 | **6** |
| Owns | **Airobotics**, **American Robotics**, Roboteam, Sentrycs, Apeiro Motion |

**Two consequences.**

1. **The Digital Bat win is much bigger than it looked.** A ~50-person Israeli company with ~$15M
   raised beat a **$5.26B** public roll-up for a national MoD platform contract. **That is
   evidence about how this buyer procures** — it selected the specialist over the scale — and it
   is directly relevant to us (`SwarmOps_Positioning_and_Pitch.md` §8.1). It is also a warning:
   eyesAtop is a stronger competitor than its headcount suggests.
2. **Airobotics is no longer an independent Israeli competitor.** It is a business unit of a US
   public company, alongside a counter-UAS line. Update the competitive census accordingly.

## 3. FlytBase growth — the question is answered, and the answer is not encouraging for the category

`SwarmOps_Positioning_and_Pitch.md` §7.1 flagged this as the unknown that changes how the TAM
slide reads: *is FlytBase's $10.8M growing 5% or 60%?*

**Headcount, across sources [SOURCED]:**

| Date | Employees |
|---|---|
| Sept 2025 | **98** |
| May 2026 | **48** — reported as −23% YoY |

Revenue reporting conflicts too: **$10.8M ARR (2025)** against **$4M (2026)** from another
aggregator. Funding is reported as **$1.62M** and **$156.5M** by different databases.

**[MODELLED] reading, with the uncertainty stated:** the databases disagree enough that no revenue
figure here is reliable. **But headcount roughly halving over eight months is not the shape of a
company growing 60%.** The leading independent pure-play in our category appears to be
**contracting, not compounding.**

**What this does to our story — and it cuts both ways, so say both:**

- **Against us:** it is the strongest evidence yet for the risk `SwarmOps_Market_Sizing.md` §2.2
  already names — that operators may not perceive this problem as software-shaped yet. **A
  shrinking category leader is a real warning and we should not explain it away.**
- **For us:** it also means the civil pure-play position is *weakening*, not consolidating —
  which is consistent with the defense-first decision, and with §1's finding that the money in
  this sector goes to whoever sells the coordination layer into a buyer that is actually
  procuring.

**Do not state FlytBase's revenue as a fact in any deck.** State the headcount trajectory, which
is consistently reported, and let it carry the point.

## 4. Military drone market discrepancy — resolved

Open item since `SwarmOps_Market_Sizing.md` §8.9. Both figures are MarketsandMarkets; they are
**two different reports with two different market definitions**, not an error. [SOURCED]

| Report | Scope | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| **Narrow** | Military drone market, conventional definition | **$15.80B (2025) → $22.81B (2030), 7.6% CAGR** |
| **Broad** | Segmented by platform (small/tactical/strategic), application (UCAV, ISR, delivery), type, MTOW, speed, operation and launch mode, propulsion | **$34.85B (2026) → $109.22B (2031), 25.7% CAGR** |

**Recommendation: cite the narrow one, and name it as the narrow one.** The broad figure is not
wrong, but a 25.7% CAGR on a military hardware market invites the question of what is inside it,
and we do not want to be defending someone else's segmentation. Either way, **state which
definition is in use** — quoting one without the other is how the 5× gap becomes our credibility
problem instead of theirs.

## 5. The funder map — who actually writes these cheques in Israel

[SOURCED]

**Defense-focused funds active in Israel:** **Protego Ventures** (Israel-based, defense-focused,
early-growth), **Aliya Capital Partners**, Silent Ventures, Lux Capital, Ora Global, NeverLift VC,
Mana, Impatient Ventures, New Vista, Entree Capital, TLV Partners, Redseed Ventures, PSG Equity,
StepStone.

**Angels named in this space:** Eyal Waldman, Jeff Horing, Yasmin Lukatz, David Chinn, Steve
Blank, and founders from Anduril and Erebor.

**Market conditions, and they are unusually favourable:**

- **Israeli defense-tech startups working with the MoD raised ~$3B in H1 2026 — three times the
  whole of 2025.**
- **Defense-tech and dual-use took ~30% of the $8.4B raised across all Israeli private hi-tech in
  H1 2026.**

**The one thing to know before approaching the obvious names.** **Protego Ventures and Aliya
Capital Partners co-led XTEND's $30M Series B extension.** They are the most defense-fluent money
in the market *and* they are already invested in a company we compete alongside. **They will know
eyesAtop, XTEND and Digital Bat before we open our mouths.** That is an argument for going to them
with the competitive census on the table (`SwarmOps_Competitive_Battlecard.md`), not for avoiding
them — but walking in without it would be fatal.

## 6. Agriculture — upgraded from unsourced to partially sourced

`SwarmOps_Israel_Drone_Landscape.md` §3.2 records agriculture as a total gap, with a note that
spraying "appears to require approval beyond the basic commercial licence [SOURCED, weakly]".
**That is now properly sourced** [SOURCED — Hebrew agri/training sources]:

- Commercial drone operation requires a **Ministry of Transport licence**.
- **Spray operations additionally require a spray certificate (תעודת רסס) from the Ministry of
  Agriculture.** Two separate approvals, two ministries.
- The **Israel Innovation Authority, with the Ministry of Agriculture, is examining purchase
  subsidies** for agricultural drone buyers — conditional on Ministry approval, applied for by the
  end customer.
- **DJI Agras T-series** is sold into Israel through DJI Enterprise Israel.

**Still missing, and it is the part that matters:** how many agricultural drones operate in
Israel, how many hold spray certificates, and who operates them. **The dual-certificate
requirement supports the earlier inference that this population is small and concentrated** — good
for enumeration, bad for volume. **Keep agriculture out of any sized segment until the certificate
holder count exists.** The Ministry of Agriculture is the single address for that number and
nobody has asked it.
