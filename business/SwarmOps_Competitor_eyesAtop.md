# Competitor Profile — eyesAtop / **Aitan** (product: **"Ronen"**)

**Internal working document, opened 2026-08-19.** Deep profile of the single closest competitor
SwarmOps has, in any market. Referenced from `SwarmOps_Competitive_Battlecard.md` §4.7 and §1.

**The most important line in this document, first:** in the field the system is not called
"eyesAtop." The company was **rebranded Aitan** and the product soldiers use is the **"Ronen"
operating system**. If you encountered this in service, *that* is the name you would have heard.

Source discipline as elsewhere: **[SOURCED]** / **[MODELLED]** / **[ASSUMPTION]** / **[PRIMARY]**.

**The name.** "Aitan" references **Captain Eitan Itzhak Oster, 22**, Udi Oster's brother, killed
**2 October 2024** in a Hezbollah ambush in southern Lebanon — the first casualty of Israel's
ground operation there. The Defense Ministry later renamed the **Iron Beam** laser interception
system **"Or Eitan"** in his memory. A second thread runs through the company: **Or Levy**, an
employee since the Tapingo years, was abducted from the Nova festival on 7 October 2023 and held
in Gaza; his wife Einav was murdered that day. He was released in February 2025.

**This is not incidental background.** It is why the company has the access it has, why the story
travels, and why competing against them on sentiment in an Israeli defense room is unwinnable.
Compete on the technical boundary or not at all — and never in a way that touches this.

## 2. What "Ronen" actually is

**A software platform delivered with ground-control hardware and edge compute. They do not make
drones.** [SOURCED]

**Physical form:** a **universal remote controller with integrated handheld screens**, plus an
**edge AI compute** element. One device, many aircraft — replacing a separate controller and a
separate operator per aircraft type.

**Functional capabilities, as described by the company and press:**

- **One operator flies multiple drones of different types simultaneously.** The headline claim.
- **Integrated with 20+ different drone platform types** — surveillance, logistics, strike, ISR.
- **OEM-agnostic:** *"if an attack drone from vendor A is in operation and next month there is a
  better, cheaper one from vendor B, you can just swap them, and the system will work."*
- **Mixes classes in one mission:** directs **attack drones from a reconnaissance platform** —
  recon aircraft finds, strike aircraft engages, one interface.
- **Shared situational awareness between operators** — live feed sharing described as *"like
  clicking the screen-sharing button on Zoom."* Two operators work the same picture together.
- **Target tracking across multiple locations**, with review of historical footage alongside live.
- **Compresses the kill chain** from identification to strike **into minutes**.
- **Reduces crew from three or four soldiers to one.**
- **Centralized mission planning**, **edge-based AI for autonomous navigation**.
- Sold as modular **"mission-ready capability packages"** — border surveillance, recon for
  manoeuvring forces, heavy-lift logistics, coordinated strike. **Solution sales, not SaaS seats.**

**What is never mentioned in any source about the product:** battery state, endurance, range
feasibility, charging, or refusing an infeasible task. Absent from every description we have found.
[This is an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence — but it is consistent across six
independent sources.]

weapon to **the operating system controlling it**; *"locking in to one vendor with one platform is
something that in today's world is very difficult."* Long-term vision: a **"ghost squad"** — two
operators running multiple autonomous robotic systems, replacing a company-sized unit. Market
positioning distinct from Anduril: rather than selling hardware to allied states, offer **non-US
militaries without R&D infrastructure a complete recon or strike solution built from the best
available platforms globally.**

## 4. What this means for SwarmOps — honestly

### 4.1 They break our main argument

`SwarmOps_Competitive_Battlecard.md` §3.1 argues every serious player is hardware-anchored or
OS-anchored, so their coordination stops at their own fleet. **eyesAtop/Aitan is the exception:**
no aircraft, no OS on the airframe, genuinely cross-vendor, and fielded at scale. **Do not use the
§3.1 argument in any Israeli defense room.** It holds against Skydio, XTEND, Auterion, Flock and
Percepto. It does not hold here.

### 4.1a But they have not reached the tier we are aiming at — [PRIMARY], §5

**A founder who flew drones in an infantry unit in late 2025 never encountered any unified
multi-drone control system, in his unit or in others.** Ronen's own strongest sourced claim is
deployment across **reconnaissance** units. **Strong in the specialist tier, absent from the
line-unit COTS-quad tier** — which is the fat part of the distribution
(`SwarmOps_Israel_Drone_Landscape.md` §1.3a) and the market SwarmOps is built for. See §5 for the
full reasoning, the claim-by-claim comparison, and the limits on how far this can be pushed.

**Structural reason it may stay that way:** Ronen's proposition is *one operator replaces three or
four* — it compresses a heavy multi-aircraft crew. The line-unit tier is the opposite shape:
**many separate soldiers, one aircraft each, across many units** (Filter B in
`SwarmOps_Market_Sizing.md` §3.1). Making a crew smaller does not coordinate a hundred independent
single-aircraft operators. **Different problem, not just a different customer.**

### 4.2 What is still ours

1. **Battery feasibility.** Absent from every description of Ronen across six sources. Still the
   only claim nobody else is making. **It is now our entire technical differentiation in defense,
   which is a thin place to stand — build it and demo it, or find a different buyer.**
2. **Different objective function.** Ronen optimizes the **kill chain**: identification → strike,
   in minutes, with a human flying. SwarmOps optimizes **fleet workload over hours**: who takes
   which task, in what order, on the battery available, unattended. Related but not the same
   problem. **Say it once, precisely, and move on** — we now make this same distinction against
   XTEND, Auterion *and* Aitan, and a line drawn three times is a positioning weakness.
3. **Their national mandate is the attack fleet.** Digital Bat is loitering/strike — explicitly
   outside SwarmOps's scope (`SwarmOps_Israel_Drone_Landscape.md` §0). But their **fielded** product
   already covers surveillance and ISR, so this is a boundary of the contract, not of the company.
   **Do not lean on it hard.**

### 4.3 What they have that we cannot get

**500,000 hours of live IDF combat operational data**, and the company names it as the moat. We
cannot buy, build or simulate that. Any pitch implying we would out-perform them on autonomy or
operational fit in an IDF context is not credible.

### 4.4 The realistic postures

**Sequencing decision, 2026-08-19: defense first, public safety as the fast reference customer**
(`SwarmOps_Positioning_and_Pitch.md` §8). Their presence does not close the field — the Israeli
defense establishment runs parallel evaluations across many vendors and re-runs categories
(20 counter-drone technologies tested in one process; XTEND won an earlier multi-drone-OS round,
eyesAtop won the next). **Competition here is not exclusion.** Within that:

- **Go at the tier they have not reached** (§4.1a) — line units, one aircraft per operator, no
  system today. **This is the primary route.**
- **Under an open architecture.** Kela's platform is explicitly advertised as modular — integrating
  new capability **without replacing core systems**. Battery-feasible planning as a component is a
  coherent proposal to that architecture rather than a competitor to it.
- **Public safety in parallel, as the fast reference.** Police, fire, critical infrastructure —
  Aitan is defense-facing and absent there, and DJI is legal and vendor-supported in civil use, so
  the military-use terms problem (`SwarmOps_Competitive_Battlecard.md` §2.8) disappears. Reference
  customer and revenue while the defense cycle runs, **not a replacement for it.**
- **Not: head-to-head on one-operator-many-drones.** That is their sentence, with half a million
  combat flight hours behind it.

## 5. First-hand check — a **negative** observation, and it is the useful kind

**[PRIMARY — Tony Verin, infantry, ~December 2025, recorded 2026-08-19]: he flew drones himself in
service and never encountered any unified multi-drone control system — not in his own unit and not
in the other units he saw. The aircraft were DJI and Autel (Mavic, Matrice, EVO), each with its own
OEM controller and its own operator.**

This is the same observer and the same bounded account as
`SwarmOps_Israel_Drone_Landscape.md` §1.3a, extended with a negative finding. **Negative
observations from a qualified observer are worth more here than a sighting would have been**,
because the press claims are quantitative and this one bounds them.

**Corroborated three times over, 2026-08-19 — and this is the part that matters.** Tony put the
question to:

- **an officer** from his own unit — no coordination system, has not heard of "Ronen";
- **a drone pilot** from his own unit — same;
- **a drone specialist described as one of the IDF's leading experts** — **also had not heard of
  "Ronen."**

Four observers now, including Tony himself. **The fourth one is not another data point of the same
kind — it changes the shape of the finding.**

1. **Four independent observers, three roles, one expert.** The §7.6 request in
   `SwarmOps_Israel_Drone_Landscape.md` — get more first-hand accounts — is now substantially
   answered, and every one of them answered the same way.
2. **It is current, not historical.** Tony's own account was ~Dec 2025. The rest is **August 2026**.
   The gap is not a wartime artifact that has since been closed; **it is open now.**
3. **"Wrong unit" no longer explains it.** A pilot not recognising the name is expected if it is not
   fielded there. An officer not recognising it is a stronger signal. **A leading IDF drone
   specialist not recognising it cannot be explained by unit assignment at all** — that is precisely
   the person whose job is to know what is fielded across the force.

### 5.0a The alternative hypothesis — take this seriously before celebrating

**A leading expert not recognising the name is at least as likely to mean the name is wrong as to
mean the system is absent.** Three readings, and we cannot yet distinguish them:

- **(A) The system is narrower than the press implies.** eyesAtop is fielded in specific
  reconnaissance and specialist units and simply has not reached the wider force. Consistent with
  everything in §5.1.
- **(B) It carries a different designation in service.** "eyesAtop" is the company, "Aitan" is a
  September-2025 corporate rebrand, and "Ronen" comes from press reporting. **The IDF may know this
  capability under an entirely different name** — programme designation, unit nickname, or the name
  of the aircraft it ships on. An expert would then recognise the *capability* described but not
  the *word*. **This is the reading a hostile investor will offer, and it is a reasonable one.**
- **(C) Compartmentalization.** Even a leading expert may not be read into a specific programme.

**RESOLVED, 2026-08-19 — reading (A).** Tony went back to the specialist and **described the
capability rather than the name**. **The capability was also unfamiliar.** A leading IDF drone
specialist did not recognise a plain-language description of multi-vendor multi-drone coordination,
with no product name attached. **(B) is ruled out — this is not a naming artifact — and (C) is
weakened, because compartmentalization would typically conceal a programme's name and existence,
not the general capability class.**

**The finding stands: the gap is real.** And it produced something better than a confirmation —
**the specialist asked to test SwarmOps in his unit, a drone R&D unit.** That request, and what to
do about it, is in `SwarmOps_Positioning_and_Pitch.md` §9.

**The boundary still travels with it.** Four observers, one force, and nothing here touches the
Digital Bat programme or the specific reconnaissance units the sourced material names. The claim is
about what four qualified people did and did not recognise — not about eyesAtop's actual
deployment footprint, which we cannot see.

**Still bounded, and the boundary must travel with the claim:** four people, one of them broad in
scope, but nothing here establishes anything about the Digital Bat programme or about specific
reconnaissance and special units — which is where the sourced material places eyesAtop.
**The defensible sentence is: "as of August 2026 we asked four people — a drone operator, a pilot,
an officer, and one of the IDF's leading drone specialists. None described a coordination system in
use, and none recognised the leading product by name."** That is what happened, it is checkable,
and it is enough. **Do not upgrade it to "the system is not in the IDF."**

### 5.1 What it does and does not contradict

Set the claims side by side, because they are not all saying the same thing:

| Claim | Source | Compatible with the observation? |
|---|---|---|
| Deployed across **"most reconnaissance units using drones or robotics"** | Times of Israel | **Yes.** Tony was line infantry, not a reconnaissance unit. No conflict |
| **"Increasingly deployed"** across reconnaissance units, north and south | Times of Israel | **Yes.** "Increasingly" is a trajectory, not a saturation claim |
| **"Widely deployed by IDF infantry brigades"** | Calcalist | **This one is in tension.** An infantry drone operator in late 2025 saw none of it, in his unit or others |
| **500,000+ operational flight hours** | company, multiple outlets | **Yes.** Hours concentrate in a few heavy-using units; a large number is compatible with narrow distribution |

**The honest reading, and it is the one to use:** Ronen is real, fielded and heavily flown — **in
reconnaissance and specialist units**. It had **not** reached the ordinary line-infantry COTS-quad
tier as of late 2025. "Widely deployed by infantry brigades" is the weakest of the claims and it is
the one a first-hand account does not support.

**What this is not:** proof of absence. One observer, one role, one period, and units differ
enormously. **Never state that Ronen is not in the IDF's infantry** — state that a founder who
flew drones in an infantry unit in late 2025 did not encounter it, which is checkable and true.

### 5.2 Why this is good news, and it is specific

`SwarmOps_Israel_Drone_Landscape.md` §1.3a established the shape of the market: the **fat part of
the distribution is COTS quads at line-unit level**, and the exotic tier is context. This
observation says the competitor is **strong in the thin tier and absent from the fat one** — at
least as of late 2025.

That is the first evidence we have that the gap SwarmOps aims at is **not** already closed:

- The volume tier is **many single-aircraft operators across many units** — Filter B in
  `SwarmOps_Market_Sizing.md` §3.1, not Filter A. Ronen's value proposition (one operator replacing
  three or four, compressing a kill chain) **does not address that shape at all** — it makes a
  heavy multi-aircraft crew smaller. It does not coordinate a hundred separate soldiers each flying
  one Mavic.
- **Nothing coordinates that tier.** No open source describes it; the competitor best positioned to
  own it has not reached it; and a formation buying 100+ of its own drones (55th Paratroopers)
  cannot be centrally coordinated by anything.

**This is the sharpest version of the SwarmOps thesis available, and it now has a first-hand
observation under it rather than an inference.**

### 5.3 What to go get — the questions that raise the value further

Worth recording in writing, with dates and role attached, from Tony **and** from Guy and Valfish
(`SwarmOps_Israel_Drone_Landscape.md` §7.6 already asks for this and it is still the cheapest
research available):

- **The load-bearing one:** when several aircraft from different units were up over the same area,
  **what stopped them colliding, and who decided who covered what?** No open source answers this.
  A first-hand answer — even "nothing, you shouted on the radio" — is the single most valuable
  sentence this company could own.
- How were tasks assigned between operators in a unit — who decided which aircraft went where?
- What happened when a battery ran low mid-task? Who tracked that, and how?
- Was any software involved at all beyond the OEM app, or was it maps, radio and voice?
- Did any *other* unit type appear to be running something different?

**Two constraints.** Record exactly as §1.3a does: attributed, dated, role stated, bounded to one
observer. And **never write down anything classified** — if it is not in an open source, it does
not go in a document or a deck.

### 5.4 Identifiers, kept for the other two founders

If Guy or Valfish are asked the same questions, these are what "Ronen" would have looked like:

1. **The name "Ronen" (רונן)** — how the system is known in units. Not "eyesAtop," not "Aitan"
   (a Sept-2025 corporate rebrand, not a field name).
2. **One controller, several aircraft types** — a single handheld flying more than one class of
   drone, rather than each aircraft arriving with its own OEM controller and operator.
3. **Integrated handheld screens** on the controller.
4. **Live feed sharing between operators**, Zoom-style screen sharing.
5. **Recon aircraft cueing a strike aircraft through the same interface.**
6. **Crew reduction** — one soldier doing what three or four did.

## 6. Open questions

1. **How does Ronen achieve 20+ platform integrations** — control-link level, vendor SDKs, or

## 7. Source conflicts — do not paper over these

- **Founding year:** 2015 [one source] vs **2017** [two sources, with founder names and location].
  Prefer 2017.
- **When Oster/Almog took over:** "weeks into the October 7 onslaught" [Times of Israel] vs
  **$1M invested March 2024, full control September 2025** [Calcalist, Ynet]. The latter is
  specific and dated — prefer it, and read the ToI phrasing as when they became *involved*.
- **Founder name spellings** vary across sources (Ido/Idan Yitzhak/Itzhak, Barkay/Barkai). Use the
  Hebrew sources if this ever needs to be exact.
- **Money:** **~$15M raised** against **"tens of millions of dollars in annual sales."** Both are
  reported; they are hard to hold together for a 50-person company, and one may be an order
  backlog, a contract value, or a translation artifact. **Do not quote the revenue figure without
  the caveat.**
