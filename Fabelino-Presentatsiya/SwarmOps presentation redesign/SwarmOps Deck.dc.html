THE PROBLEM

Flying one drone takes a soldier’s full attention

A pilot in the air is a rifle out of the fight While a soldier flies manually, the team is effectively one member short.
Two teams, two drones, zero coordination Neighboring units fly the same sector without knowing about each other.
Capacity capped at one aircraft No operator ever realizes their full capability flying a single drone.

THE COST

Manual control doesn’t scale

More drones → More operators → More errors → Degraded missions
+15% wasted flight time from suboptimal tasking  (illustrative estimate)

ARCHITECTURE

Six services, one gateway

Small pieces instead of one big program. Each can fail and recover on its own.

gateway the only public door, routes every request
auth log-in, tokens and roles
fleet the drone registry: state, battery, payload
mission orders and their lifecycle, creation to completion
planning the brain: assigns drones to missions, replans in flight
telemetry live positions and battery, streamed off the bus
notify pushes alerts to operators the moment they matter
RabbitMQ message bus services talk through queues, never directly
drone simulator virtual drones feeding the bus

NETWORKING

SECURITY

Military doctrine, applied to the cloud

PERIMETER DEFENSE One guarded checkpoint Like a base with a single gate: only the load balancer is public. Every service sits in private subnets, invisible from the internet.
NEED TO KNOW Least privilege, per service Each service has its own identity and only the permissions its job requires. A breached service can’t reach beyond its role.
OPSEC No standing secrets You don’t leave codes lying around. Credentials are short-lived and issued at runtime; there is nothing permanent to steal.
STANDING ORDERS Git is the order of the day Any hand-made change on a server is unauthorized drift. Argo CD detects it and restores what’s written in git, within minutes.

NEXT  Integration into closed military networks. The doctrine holds; only the perimeter moves.
